Summary

Method-to-profile matching framework and hard-won technique rules — grind, contact time, yield loss, freshness, equipment quirks

Details
Method-to-profile matching
Washed and fruity-fermented coffees → V60 (clarity highlights florals and bright fruit acidity)
Heavy-body naturals → French press or moka (immersion complements density)
Honey-process coffees → moka (short extraction avoids bitterness from prolonged immersion); avoid honey process in French press
Declared flavor notes carry more weight than processing label when choosing brew method — e.g. a washed coffee with heavy sweet-spiced notes may perform better in moka than V60
Extraction rules
Delicate/frutal Café Quindío coffees in French press require shorter contact time and coarser grind than heavier profiles — the opposite of the Mild Honey approach
Mild Honey works with slightly longer contact because its inherent sweetness provides a buffer; Berry Bloom and Geisha have no such buffer and need extraction minimized
Fixed grind rule: if the coffee bed begins visibly clogging during the first or second pour with a fixed grind, abandon the filter method rather than continue adjusting recipe variables. Alternatives: moka, AeroPress (short immersion, 85°C, slow press), or cold brew
Grinder handling
On the Timemore C2S, more clicks = coarser grind (not finer) — this has caused errors in prior sessions when assumed the reverse
When switching methods, always grind from finer to coarser (V60 first, then French press) to avoid fine particle contamination
Cleaning the Timemore's central shaft (not just the burrs) resolved grind consistency issues
Diagnosing sediment and yield
Sediment in French press can result from three compounding causes: inconsistent grind (boulders + fines) from residue in the Timemore's central shaft; incomplete skimming leaving particles on inner glass walls; and plunger misalignment
Actual liquid yield loss in the user's French press consistently runs 15–20% of water loaded (higher than the theoretical 12–15%) — recipes should account for this by loading extra water upfront
Freshness and instruction clarity
V60 grind degrades noticeably even within an hour (high surface area exposure); moka is more forgiving — same-day use acceptable, next-morning tolerable if stored airtight/opaque. Best practice: grind within 30–60 seconds of brewing
Recipe language like "revolver 30–40 segundos" means continuous stirring for that duration starting at 0:00 — not waiting until second 30. Future recipes should avoid this ambiguity with explicit timestamps
